It’s tempting to start a new workspace by building the perfect system. A folder
for every project. A label for every kind of thought. A dashboard that has a
place for everything, before there’s anything to put in it.

Sometimes, the most useful place to start is a single note.

## Capture first. Find the shape later.

A question from a meeting. Something you want to look up. A sentence you don’t
want to lose. These don’t need a filing system before they deserve a place to
live.

Write them down. Give them a title you’ll recognize. Let a little material
collect before deciding how it belongs together.

## Let connections do some of the work.

When two notes start to speak to each other, link them. When a few belong to the
same project, bring them into a collection. If you want to see your notes and
tasks side by side, make a dashboard.

<Figure
  src="/assets/docs/ways-of-organization/dashboards.png"

/>

Each step adds a little structure when it becomes useful. There’s no need to
decide on the whole system at once.

## A small habit is enough.

Try taking a few minutes at the end of your day to revisit what you’ve captured.
Keep what matters, connect what belongs together, and leave the rest for another
time.

Your workspace doesn’t have to be finished. It just has to help you think.
